A well-written press release is the **difference** between an article on 50 newspapers and an email ignored in an editor's inbox. In this guide we walk you through, step by step, how to build one that works.

## 1. The inverted pyramid rule

The most important information (who, what, when, where, why) has to be in the **first paragraph**. The press moves fast — if the first 30 seconds don't land, the rest doesn't matter.

> **Pro tip:** Write the first paragraph so that if you cut it away from the rest of the text, it would still be a valid article on its own.

## 2. A short, clear headline, with no marketing-speak

- ❌ "We're revolutionizing the industry with a disruptive, innovative platform!"
- ✅ "U.S. startup launches the first marketplace for independent pharmacists"

The headline should say **what happened**, not sell.

## 3. The right length

**300–600 words** for a standard press release. Anything over 800 and editors start cutting or ignoring it.

## 4. Real quotes from real people

A credible quote from a founder, CEO or expert gives the article weight:

> "We launched this product because we saw that small pharmacies don't get the same terms as the big chains. It's a fairness problem, not just a business one." — Andrew Parker, founder.

## 5. Data + context

Without numbers, a press release is just an opinion. With numbers (market, customers, growth), it becomes **news**.

## 6. Closing elements

- A link to your site
- PR contact details
- A short boilerplate (2–3 sentences about the company)

## The takeaway

A good press release tells a **real story**, in **plain words**, with **concrete data** and **a human angle**. If you read it yourself and don't fall asleep, that's a good sign.
