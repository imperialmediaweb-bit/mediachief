Most people who send us an article have never written one before. It shows less than they fear, and the gap is almost always structure rather than style. Here is the structure.

Set aside forty minutes. You will not need all of it.

## Before you start: one sentence

Write the thing that happened, in one sentence, as if telling a friend who does not work with you.

> *We opened a second workshop, in Austin.*
> *The book comes out on the 14th.*
> *We now deliver the same day anywhere in the county.*

If you cannot do this, the problem is not the writing. Something has to have happened — a launch, a change, a number, a date, a first. "We exist and we are good at our job" is true of everyone and is not news.

## The headline

Say what happened, plainly, in under about twelve words. No company name unless the name is the news.

Good: *Austin workshop opens as Marchetti doubles repair capacity*
Weak: *Marchetti Announces Exciting Expansion News*

Write it last if it will not come. Most people find their headline somewhere in paragraph three.

## The first paragraph

Who, what, where, when — in two or three sentences. Assume the reader stops after this, because many will, and make sure they still got the news.

> *Marchetti Restoration opened a second workshop in Austin this week, adding six benches and cutting the wait for structural repairs from four weeks to ten days. The business has worked on furniture from the 1800s onwards since 2011.*

## The middle: three paragraphs, three jobs

**The detail.** What exactly is new. Numbers if you have them — a count, a date, a price, a distance. Specifics are what separate a release from an advert.

**The quote.** One, from a real person, with their actual job title. Make it say something a press release would not say on its own: why this was difficult, why now, what you expected to be wrong. Avoid *"We are thrilled to announce."* Everyone is thrilled. It is the least informative sentence in English.

**The context.** Why it matters to somebody outside the company. Who this helps, what it replaces, what was impossible before.

## The end

Two or three sentences on who you are: founded when, where, what you do, how many people. Then where to go — your site, the product page, the booking page.

## Then put it down

Read it again after an hour. Cut every sentence that would still be true if you deleted it. That is usually a quarter of the text, and the piece gets better each time.

## Length

300 to 800 words. Under 300 reads thin, over 800 is rarely read to the end, and neither helps you.
