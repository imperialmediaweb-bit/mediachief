Press releases are **the most effective source of diversified editorial links** for U.S. websites — if they're done right. Here's how.

## Anatomy of an SEO-friendly press release

### 1. The headline (H1 on the published site)

- Contains the **main keyword** naturally
- Maximum 70 characters so it doesn't get truncated
- The tone should be editorial, not a direct sales pitch

### 2. The first paragraph

- Repeats the keyword **once**, naturally
- Contains your brand + a clear value proposition
- Length: 2–3 sentences

### 3. Internal links

A press release ideally has **2 links maximum** back to your site:
- One with a **brand anchor** (the company name)
- One with a **generic anchor** ("for more details", "learn more")

> **Do not** add 5–6 links with exact-match anchors — Google flags schemes like that instantly.

## Common mistakes

1. **Over-optimizing anchors** — the same anchor text on all 50 sites raises automatic flags.
2. **Identical duplicate content** — 100% identical copies across 50 domains is a PBN signal. We partially rewrite to avoid that.
3. **Links to weak internal pages** — if you point backlinks at a page with no content, you lose most of the value.
4. **A release that's purely an offer** — Google doesn't promote disguised advertising. A press release must carry **informational value**.

## What not to expect

- Rankings **do not** change in 7 days. The effect shows at **2–4 months**.
- Not all links stay "dofollow" long term — that's normal.
- A single press release doesn't make up for a site with major technical problems.

## Conclusion

SEO through press releases **works in 2026** if you:
- **Write well** (editorial content, not disguised advertising)
- **Distribute widely** (50 domains > 1 big domain)
- **Repeat consistently** (1 release/month or more, via a subscription)
- **Monitor** (PDF report + an SEO audit after 3 months)
