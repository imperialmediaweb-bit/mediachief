Someone asked this under one of our ads, which told us we had been using the phrase as if everybody already knew it. Here is the plain version.

## The short answer

A wire service is a company that takes your press release and pushes it out to a network of publishers, databases and journalists. You write once; they distribute. The name comes from the telegraph wires that moved news between newsrooms a century ago, and the business has kept the word long after the wires went away.

The best known are PR Newswire, Business Wire, GlobeNewswire and EIN Presswire. There are dozens of smaller ones.

## What you are actually buying

Three different things get sold under one word, and it is worth separating them.

**Reach into newsrooms.** Your release lands in systems that reporters and editors watch. Most releases are ignored — that is not a scandal, it is arithmetic, because far more are sent than anyone can read. But a genuinely newsworthy one can be picked up and written about, and that pickup is worth more than anything you can buy.

**Syndicated placements.** Your release appears, usually verbatim, on sites that republish wire content automatically. This is the part most buyers think they are getting, and it is the part that varies most between services.

**A record.** A permanent, dated, public page saying this was announced on this day. Useful for investors, partners, due diligence, and anyone who wants to check you exist.

## What it costs

Pricing changes often, so check before you believe anyone, including us. The shape of it is stable, though: national distribution on a major wire is typically a few hundred dollars for a single release, and the price climbs with word count, images, video, extra regions and industry targeting. Budget services sit lower, and what falls away first is the newsroom reach.

The thing to watch is that most wires charge **per release**. Two announcements, two fees.

## Where we fit

We are not a wire. We publish your article as an article on 50 US newspapers we operate, one in every state, and we give you a report with every link.

That difference matters in both directions, and you should know both.

**What you get that a wire does not give you:** a real article page per outlet, written for that outlet rather than copy-pasted, with links that stay up permanently. You get the addresses, so you can check every one and send the list to anyone who asks.

**What a wire gives you that we do not:** the chance of being picked up by a newsroom that is not ours. If your news is genuinely of national interest, that possibility is worth paying for, and we would tell you to pay for it.

## The honest version of when to use which

If you have hard news — funding, an acquisition, a product that changes something, a result somebody is waiting for — a major wire is a reasonable spend, because you are buying a lottery ticket on a journalist picking it up.

If what you have is a story worth telling but not national news — a book, a local business, a milestone, a service, a founder worth knowing about — a wire will mostly give you a page on the wire's own site and a scattering of syndication. For that case, fifty articles you can point at, for $149, is a better trade.

What neither of us can sell you is attention. We publish the article and give you the links. Whether it brings visitors or customers depends on the story, and anyone who promises otherwise is selling something they cannot deliver.
